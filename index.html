Hello! Welcome to my page! My name is Simha (or Lakshminarasimhan if you're feeling adventurous). I'm a food scientist working mainly on food proteins, using them to create delicious food structures.

01. About

My work over the past decade has focussed on alternative proteins, connecting their physical chemistry, extraction, and colloidal properties. I'm also interested in food rheology, interfacial behaviour, and broader multiphase material science. Increasingly I'm using machine learning tools, as they become more accessible, to make my science broader, and I aim to create open-access tools. My research ambition is to create tools and insights to help uncover the galaxy of protein properties that create food textures.

I have a Bachelor's degree in Chemical Engineering from India, where I'm originally from. I have an MSc in Food Technology from Wageningen University. I also have a PhD in Food Science from Wageningen University, working on "Pea proteins as structuring agents in edible soft materials." After my PhD I worked as a plant protein scientist at Planted Foods in Switzerland for two years, before winning a Marie Curie grant to work at the University of Leeds, where I now research the link between protein properties and how they feel in your mouth.

02. Research

Ongoing · Marie Curie, Leeds

PROTSENS

Built a computational pipeline that pairs scattering data with physics-informed ML to go looking for new plant protein surfactants, part of my broader work on why some plant proteins feel unpleasant in your mouth and others don't. Preprint currently under review at Communications Chemistry.

2017–2021 · PhD

Pea proteins as structuring agents

Four years of proving that pea protein doesn't need to be heavily purified to work well in emulsions, gels, and 3D-printed edible materials. The messy version holds its own.
